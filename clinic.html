Fiber Optic Stress Testing, Automated Test Bench

Our AMD-sponsored clinic team is characterizing light transmission properties in optical fibers. My role is to develop the hardware that makes those stresses repeatable. Work is done under NDA. 

MY ROLE
Test Engineer — bench design, build & test execution
SPONSOR
Advanced Micro Devices, San Jose
INSTRUMENTS
LabJack T4 DAQ, polarization synthesizer, Thorlabs polarimeter, manual controllers
MEASURES
Polarization Dependent Loss & Mode Dispersion in fiber links

The question

I worked with AMD engineers to define realistic field stressors, then designed a modular benchtop test bed to reproduce them under control.

The test bench

Closed-loop temperatureA chiller (10–60 °C, ±0.01 °C) runs water through cooling pipes in the chamber, with a PWM fan and thermistor feedback controlled from the lab PC.
Sensing & data acquisitionThermistors and an airflow sensor feed the DAQ; a vibration sensor runs through a microcontroller. Everything logs to .csv / .json.
Polarization optical pathA 1310 nm laser passes through the connector, polarizer and polarization controller into the fiber under test, then out to the polarimeter.

Test method

I execute benchtop procedures and a design of experiments across multiple rounds, varying fiber length and cable management under each stressor. Polarization synthesizers, polarimeters and manual controllers measure polarization-dependent loss (PDL) and polarization mode dispersion (PMD), and I analyze transmission and polarization data to tie each stressor to signal degradation.

Fixture design Instrumentation Design of experiments Data analysis

Where it’s headed

The goal is to not only build a modular, compact test bench with repeatable stressors, but also to show which stress conditions drive the most signal degradation, backed by repeatable test data. The project is ongoing through the 2026–27 academic year.
